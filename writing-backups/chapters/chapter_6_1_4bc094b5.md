# Chapter 6.1

\---


“Gods below! How did they manage to give me my legs back but also leave me with six years of atrophy?” Cas groaned as they walked along the dirt road. He rubbed at his aching thighs in a vain hope that it could do anything to get rid of the ache that had begun screaming at him as soon as the adrenaline had worn off from the fight.


“Um, ‘gods below’? Are you, like, some kind of Satanist?” Sawyer asked, giving him a bit of an odd look.


“Satanist? Oh!” Cas burst out laughing as soon as he understood the confusion. “Naw, it’s a turn of phrase in a book I used to read about a 2000-year-old druid in modern dayArizona. Good book!” It was a great book too, it’d got him through some of the hardest times in his attempted rehab and then the early days of his post-paralysis.


“Huh! Not sure I ever heard of it, whenever I read, I would usually just read webnovels or fanfics and what not, you know? Oh! I did read one super old series a couple years ago, some kid with a scar in magic school?” Replied Sawyer.


Cas shook his head at the idea of zoomer kids calling Harry Potter a ‘super old series’. “Please don’t talk like that, I’m not even thirty, you’re going to make me want to shrivel up and become a crotchety curmudgeon all over again!”


”Uuuuuh… and using the phrase ‘*crotchety curmudgeon’… **doesn’t*** make you exactly that?”


The two laughed and continued to share idle small talk as they continued down the dirt trail.


\---


After about a half hour later, the view finally changed as they broke through the trees. The two companions had already been smelling the aroma for a few minutes before its source finally came into sight. For this first time in what felt like days their feet finally stopped moving and they just stared, utterly dumbfounded at what they saw.


A couple hundred meters ahead of where they stood, the path terminated at none other than… a food truck??


A few dozen people arrayed themselves in a line up to the window of the bright yellow truck, one of them peeling off from the front in a regular rhythm to walk around behind it where a long waist high fence partitioned off a wide, bustling picnic grounds-style eating area filled with even more people at one of the many wooden picnic tables. The sight was bizarre in just how utterly *normal* it was.


Before they could think to question any of it, their stomachs growled audibly to remind them of how long it had been since either of them had eaten anything, and how much physical activity they had done since waking up in this weird place. As if their feet had a will of their own, they found themselves in the line before they could think.


By the time they got to the front of the line, Cas could barely think from the hunger, and he had to bring his hand to the corner of his mouth to see if he had been drooling. It wasn’t just the hunger either. The food smelled *intoxicating*. That didn’t mean he hadn’t taken the time to read his surroundings though. The truck looked like a classic mexican food truck from Earth, *Am I still on Earth?* He thought to himself. The mustard yellow paint was well worn at the corners but still vibrant, showing years of love put into keeping it in tip-top shape. The bottom third of the truck was painted in shamrock green topped by a line of red triangles sandwiched between two lines of the same red wrapping around the length of the truck.


And then the chef came to the window, and all thoughts fled from his mind. Leaning out the window and looking down at him with a genuine smile was what could only be described as a goat. And honest to gods ***goat***.


If a goat was a six foot tall ungulate with as a bipedal ungulate standing over *six feet tall* with four fingers and an opposable thumb on each hand. He had no idea why, but for a moment his hands were fixated on the goat-man’s fingers. Instead of fingernails like a human, the goat-man’s fingernails were thick and black, a stark contrast to the goat-man’s soft white fur. It looked as if they had evolved from the hard horn on the hooves of any earth goat.


”…there friend?”


“Pardon?” Cas asked. A voice startled him out of his stunned silence and made him look up. The goat man had been talking to him. The ***goat man*** had been ***talking*** to him. *Yup. I’m either dead, on a **ton** of drugs, or aliens are definitely real. I’m not sure which option I’d prefer right about now.* Cas thought to himself.


”I was asking you if I could help you there friend?” The goat man asked again in a jovial tone.


“Um…” Cas had no idea what he was supposed to ask. Did he order food? Just pretend like this was any normal food truck not in the middle of nowhere in what could very well be some sort of pocket dimension?


”Take it easy friend, it’s all new and I’d wager you’re quite overwhelmed by all this.” Cas only realized now that the goat-man’s voice had, of all things, a french accent?! “Let’s start small. I’m Jaques, and this is my truck.” He pointed above his head and Cas looked up to see a sign that read “Jacque's Creole-Mongol-Aztec fusion tacos” in bright bold letters framed by a boardwalk caricature portrait of the *goat*\-man holding up a thumb and winking.


”let me know what you want to order, and then you can go back to the picnic area to eat. It’ll perk you up, and your agent will join you shortly. They’ll be able to explain everything to you, so don’t you worry!”


With no mental energy left to properly process any of that, all Cas could say was “but I don't have any money on me.”


The goat-man, *Jacques*, laughed heartily, the tail end of each chuckle holding the littlest hint of a bleat. He extended his hand out to me, his welcoming smile not changing. “Don't you worry about that, friend! Just a solid handshake, that's the bill!”


Hesitant, Cas reached out and took Jacques hand, exchanging a pleasantly firm handshake only thrown off by the *small* detail that the hand in this was covered in a fine white fur.


“Quite the handshake you've got there! I know just what to give you, friend, don't you worry. Here's your chit, hand it to Tim round back and he'll have your platter ready for you!”


Daunted by the prospect of meeting another goat-man, Cas waited just off to the side as Sawyer went through the same process. The kid walked towards him afterwards staring at his hand, but instead of Cas’ primary air of confusion, Sawyer's confusion was clearly being overpowered by wonder. He wasn't sure why to be honest, but it made him smile. Patting the kid on the shoulder, he spoke up with a small chuckle. “Come on bud, let's get some food eh?”


(Mostly) brought out of his trance, Sawyer nodded and plodded along behind him as they passed through the fence door into the picnic yard beyond.

After the friendly robot with what looked like an oversized soda can for a head handed them their red plastic food trays, they headed for an empty picnic table out on the field.


“What's with the face dude? “ Cas asked with a chuckle, as Sam made a face like his stomach was starting to turn.


“This food doesn’t concern you *at all?* There's bugs in it!” Sam replied while looking down at the platter in his hands.


”Not at all haha besides, That doesn’t even have bugs in it, that's just a quesadilla!” Cas said, looking at the platter.


Sitting down opposite each other at an empty picnic bench, Cas looked over his bounty of a meal. It felt like ages since he’d last eaten anything, and frankly it had been. In total honesty, he hadn’t been taking good care of his health for the last couple of years as his depression worsened. The last thing he’d remembered eating was a microwaved breakfast burrito from costco the morning be’d been abducted by this whole game thing. *If my mental math is correct, I think that means I haven't eaten in more than 24 hours, at the very least?*


So Jaques the sentient goat man had been correct, he really had known just what would hit the spot. A little flag at the top of the platter read “The ‘I can’t make up my mind’ platter! For the player who can’t help but want just a little bit of everything!” Followed by a caricature of an enthusiastic Jaques giving a thumbs up.


A smaller portion of every item on the menu lay arrayed out in front of him; not just ribs, tacos, and mini bites, but also servings of each of the drink options *and* both desserts. Yes, some of them had bugs in them, but everyone was saying that was the food of the future anyways and it did look so damn delicious.


He barely waited a long breath before digging in with gusto. It did NOT disappoint. He lost himself in the food, barely managing to pace himself to avoid bringing it back up just as fast as it had gone down. This was an endurance race not a sprint, and Cas was happy to take his time because he was the winner one way or another.


“So… how long do you think he’ll be checked out, kiddo?” Said a male voice with a vintage New Yorker accent that Cas didn't recognize. Looking around, Cas couldn't see anyone walking towards them in the direction the voice came from.


“too high kid, down here.”


Cas looked down and was once again stunned. To his credit, it was not for long, as this wasn't the first “impossible” thing he'd seen today. Standing across the table from him at roughly five feet tall was a monkey. In a suit. And parachute pants.


“Not a monkey, kid,” the brown-furred humanoid alien said to him before words could come out of his opening mouth. “yep, I know what you're thinking, you humans always default to calling any species like us monkeys. I tell ya, your education system is shit.” he said exasperatedly, then under his breath, “*Labeling every damn primate as a monkey, for Pete's sake.*”


Looking at them for a moment longer, Cas took the being in. He had long arms, longer proportionally than a human would have, each ending in large hands with long, nimble looking fingers. On second inspection, not only were the fingers of each hand incredibly long, he only had four fingers on each hand instead of five, and each of the fingers had *four joints* instead of three. The gibbon-man's face was framed with the same coppery brown fur that seemed to spread all the way down to his hands which had the same tanned pink skin of his face. All of this was almost less shocking than his outfit. A tan suit jacket over top of a loosely buttoned yet crisp white dress shirt had its sleeves pushed up to the elbows.


Topping the look off with a vintage cream white Panama hat sporting a coal black ribbon, He looked like a back alley loan shark from a movie about the roaring twenties. That was until Cas got to the pants. They weren't just any hammer pants, they were **red satin hammer pants**. Honestly, the whiplash the outfit gave him cleared his head and helped him focus on the conversation.


“ok, fair *haha*, I did think you were a monkey, but then what are you? Or is that some space-p.c. thing you aren't supposed to ask?” Cas replied, now too curious not to try and find out, even if he was breaking some taboo.


“**Ha!** You think the galaxy would get anywhere if folks had to remember every one of the thousands of sentient races moving about? I'm a Lemyr. Technically if we're using your *taxonomy*, I'd be considered a “lesser ape” like your gibbons. Criminal that they're the only species in that classification if you ask me. There's this kid on YouTube, *Casual Geography*, you've gotta watch him some day, I tell ya. Here, I'll send it to your slate, not that you have one yet.” the Lemyr said with a laugh that was some kind of mix between a hoot and a bark.

Is that something like a tablet?” Cas assumed there had to be some sort of scifi tablet computer wizzgig out there. It's not like humans could be the only species to invent smart phones and all that.

They'll even do so with the more unique terminals, like ones made for folks without your traditional three-and-one like you and I.
