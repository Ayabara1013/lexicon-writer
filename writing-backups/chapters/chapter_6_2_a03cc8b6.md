# Chapter 6.2

(possible royal road chapter break)


Did he just say *back* on earth? Cas thought to himself.


“but the most widespread type is commonly called a slate because it looks, frankly, like a plain slate grey rectangle haha” the lemyr said with another hooting laugh. “They're the most adaptable type of personal terminal out there and can slot into or communicate with basically any type of computer with an interface port. They'll even do so with the more unique terminals, like ones made for folks without your traditional three-and-one like you and I. I guess you'd be four-and-one haha” he hooted, glancing at Cas's hands.


Sliding a small device out of an inner breast pocket he turned it in his fingers for a moment so Cas could see. It really was just a plain, slate grey slab of metal or some other material, just about the size of a standard smartphone.


Slipping it back into his pocket, he continued. “most of the galaxy doesn't have the interface that you've been seeing, that's special to the game. It's basically a proprietary OS made by the big AI brain whatchamahoozit that runs all the background functions of the game. Once you go through the gate though, you'll have to rely on public terminals for a while. You'll be able to find one at the adventurers guild and in most settlements.”


\---


Cas and this Lemyr man continued to chat about smaller things while Sam snapped out of his trance, which took even longer after looking over and seeing a furred primate man sitting next to him and smirking through a smile filled with far-too-large canines.


Turns out the man was named Gizmo, and he was the agent Jaques the goat man had mentioned. When Cas mentioned that he had fostered a kitten named Gizmo years ago, Gizmo the agent laughed, and told him that Gizmo meant something different in his tongue. With the number of different tongues and peoples in the Milky Way, that sort of thing happened all the time so folks never got bothered by it and more often than not found it quite amusing.


He told Cas that the root of the name roughly meant “troublemaker”, or more particularly “*one who liked to cause trouble in the night*” and was given to him because when he was born, his baby fur was all black and he'd had a habit of crawling out of his crib or his mother's arms when he was supposed to be sleeping. When Cas told him that Gizmo was an all black kitten and had liked to stretch out between his arms while he was working then fall asleep and fart on him, Gizmo ***howled*** with laughter along with Sam who'd finally caught up to the rest of them.


And apparently Jaques was from a race called Urgots in galactic common, or Medra'an in their native tongue. Cas tried to pronounce the native way, but he couldn't get the *bleats* right. He failed so hard, Jaques came over and brought him a new flight of drinks as thanks for the laugh.


“Your *kyured’gozhi* is excellent! How long have you been learning *mon ami*?” Jaques joked. “Don't you worry, even our youngling kits can't properly enunciate their beats and fricatives until they enter puberty. I tell you three, *les chevreaux* are so *adorable* when they're learning their bleats, *non*?”


According to Gizmo, Kyured’gozh(i) was the endonymic name for Jaques’ people's language and homeland similar to Japan's use of nihongo and Nippon. However even on their home world, most other races could not speak similarly to them either so they had been using Medra'an, Medra’ani, and Medra’ak for their species, language, and homeland respectively long before they were integrated into the wider galactic community.


While Gizmo was his agent, apparently Sam's agent was a piglin woman by the name of Momma Magalioni, or “Momma Mags” who would come by a little bit later to talk to Sam one on one more in depth about his class options and other busy work-type things.


…


After looking at his slate,  Gizmo turned back to both of them, now well on their way to a blissful food coma. Or so Cas would have thought, but the drinks that kept being brought to them as fast as they could chug them down seemed to have some sort of restorative properties.


“So let's see what you've got though for class options. It's my job to make sure you perform well, and I do like to think I'm actually good at my job. Let's start with you Mister Flynn, seeing as you are actually my client here at this table.


“According to the docket I've got here,” he started, gesturing at his slate,  “You've been given the standard three choices, plus an additional two for your bonus achievements in the tutorial, and one of them is even a special class, damn kid! So first things first, you need to know a bit about how classes work. While one of the classes is a special rarity class, and another happens to be mundane, a step below even common, rarity does not guarantee that a class is *stronger* than another. *Most* any class can be strong as long as you build it right.


“Next, you've got your base class types; **Fighter**, **Skirmisher**, **Mage**, **Adept**, and **Specialist**. Specialist is a bit of a different case so we'll get to that last. Fighters are what they sound like, you'd classic archetypal fighters. Think knights and barbarians, that type of deal. They are the in-your-face combatants more than any other class. That's not to say that all base class categories don't have both melee and ranged options.


“Next you have skirmishers. If fighters are the strength builds, skirmishers are the dex builds. Skirmishers are classes like rogues, rangers, and so on. They have a much broader mix of melee and ranged than fighters on average and they don't often prefer to be the main focus of enemy attacks.


“Now those two are your martial classes. The ones who prefer physical attacks and weapons. Next you've got your spellcasters, starting with the mages. Mages are usually your traditional arcane spellcasters like wizards or ritualists. Imagine throwing fireballs, creating arcane wards, and all that stuff.


After that are adepts. While similar to mages, adepts prefer to draw their power from a source, instead of shaping collected mana into spells like mages usually do. Furthermore, adept type classes are the most common support based classes. Some easy examples are clerics and druids.


Specialists are most often things like crafters, farmers, builders, and researchers. You usually find these on folks who choose not to advance quests and instead carve out their own niche. ***That said***, it is basically a catch-all for unique, outside-the-standard-mould classes, so they can technically pop up in any niche.


All of that said! A class type does not force each class to play by those certain rules and you can often get hybrid classes. For example, the Paladin, Warpriest, and Grove Warden classes are all decently common fighter-adept hybrid classes, and the Arcane Archer and Shadowblade classes are both popular skirmisher-mage hybrids. Though many folks take the Arcane Trickster class branch instead and avoid that edge lord class!” he finished with a laugh.


*It seems like edgelords transcend racial divides haha* Cas thought to himself. *He just said class evolutions, if I don't get a chance to ask about that, I'll need to look into it more in depth later!*


“Bottom line is that class type or number of types doesn't have any effect on a class's power or rarity, though the number of classes with more than two types is quite low, especially for classes that are possible to get right out of the gate.


“let's go over what selection you're being offered, honestly it's a pretty good spread. You've got the Firewatch Lookout, Computer Wiz(ard), and the Mechanic for your initial 3, then for your bonus classes you've got the “Basic-Ass Fighter” and the “Redneck Engineer”.”


## Firewatch Lookout


“This guy is pretty close to a classic skirmisher-type scout class, with a bit of a twist. You get enhancements to scouting out areas, heightened perception, danger sense, and depending on how you build it you can get some ability to dampen flames.


“The big downside is that along with having less attack moves or features that synergize with dealing damage, a large number of your scout abilities scale on your elevation / proximity to wooded areas so if you are in a deep cave or a desert, you’re running at half-mast at best. You give up the versatility of most scouts for being *the guy* in high, wooded areas.


## Computer Wiz(ard)


“Now with the Computer Wiz, it’s basically your standard wizard archetype class, with some tech mixed in. That’s not even that odd either, as tech and tech magic is literally a sub-set within the arcane magic ‘sphere’ just like wood magics would be a subset of primal magic. . Effectively anything that scales arcane also scales tech, so if you see something is tech magic, you can also see it as arcane magic, because it effectively is.”


”Quick pause there,” Cas said with a slight raise of his hand, “You mentioned arcane being a ‘magic sphere’ and primal magic as well?”


”Yeah fair, let me break that down quick for ya.” Gizmo replied. “Basically all magic can be categorized into four main ‘schools’ or ‘spheres’ of magic. Arcane, primal, divine, and occult.


”Arcane magic is what you most often think of when you think of the classic wizard, basically Merlin shit. Magic missiles, teleportation, all those sorts of things. You can basically think of arcane magic as another fundamental force in the universe alongside space and time. Gravity and what not, you know? It’s like the glue that holds it together. So in essence if you are using arcane magics, you are forming and bending the threads of the universe to your intent. It’s also probably the most self-determininistic magic type out there.


”Next is primal magic. That’s your druidry, move the oceans, shape fire, all that type of stuff. Tree hugger shit. Can be pretty good too. Primal is right up there with arcane for being the best school for ‘blaster’ type builds as you might guess, being able to throw fireballs and lightning bolts and what not. A lot of those elemental / evocation type spells have crossover between the two as well.


”Divine magic is the deific stuff. You are getting your power from a being greater than you. Priest shit. Now, we don't have to get into the ‘are gods real’ talk, all the super power cosmic juju the folks running this show have under their control is close enough, and in fact there are beings powerful and ancient enough out there in wherever that they can certainly be called gods, and will in fact grant their powers to followers. Just remember, because it’s priest shit, Doesn’t mean it’s all sunshine and rainbows although a lot of them do really get off on that ‘holy light’ business. For every fru-fru sparkle healer you find, don’t forget that there’re also gods of violence and death.


“Last I mentioned was occult. This is that weird shit. That kinda weird shit that doesn't fit into a ‘most people who use this are normal’ category. This is the ‘that weird kid in my highschool art class who brought a vial of his own blood to school probably uses this’ typeah shit, you know? Bargains for power, creepy or just downright weird rituals, maybe even a cosmic-scale endritch entity or two. Chthulu shit.”


## Mechanic


“This one’s easy enough. It’s a specialist-type adventurer class categorized as an all-rounder. You build machines that do stuff for you. It's the type of specialist class that actually does play like a traditional adventurer in most cases just like the inventor or engineer classes. In fact, the tech/ mechanical classes are the most common adventurer classes within the specialist category. The big difference between this one and the more standard inventor and engineer are that while those two are more versatile, the mechanic focuses more on building and maintaining vehicles to use as their primary tool for combat and other adventuring tasks. Don’t get me wrong, you can never go wrong with a mech suit or a tank or a starfighter, but when you’re caught in the open without one, you’ll have very few options especially early on.”


As he finished his breakdown of all the classes, Gizmo stole one of the last crispy caiman bites off Cas’s rapidly dwindling tray and popped it into his mouth before taking his time to lick off his fingers.


“All three of those are uncommon-grade classes so nothing too out of the ordinary there, but it seems like each of them have some sort of twist or specialization that narrows its focus compared to the more baseline class it was based off of. Firewatch and mechanic are the most specific of the three in their differences, meaning you’ll be more restricted there, so I would personally recommend the Computer Wizard class. The main quirk of the class isn’t as restrictive compared to the other two, and it actually creates a higher power ceiling as a tradeoff for raising the skill floor a bit. You have to program your own spells. Now, there are repositories that you can get spells out of, and you aren’t going to be forced to do so, but the cost for that higher power ceiling through being able to customize and program your spells is you actually have to do so yourself.”


Honestly, that didn’t seem like the worst tradeoff to Cas’s mind, after all he’d been a professional software engineer for years, and had basically nothing to do but sit in front of his computer for years after the accident, but it did irk him that he only seemed to be given the classes with hoops to jump through to get the most out of them. As he thought about that, Gizmo stole another caiman bite from his plate and took his time cleaning his fingers before turning to address Cas again.


“but oooh boy, let's get into those last 2.”
