# Chapter 3 \- Company

The voice spoke to him from every angle, as if it was all around him, or even inside his head.


"Who's there?! Who was that?!" he shouted, pointing his new, nearly empty gun at every shadow and bush in sight.


"You heard that? Woops! Didn't know this thing was on hehehe!" the voice giggled in response to him. It sounded like… an old grandmother? Like one of the Golden Girls? He'd actually never watched the show. As Cas thought that, he realized that he may never get the chance. Or MASH, or that new Stargate series that'd just been announced.


"I'm not supposed to talk to you until at least the second round, just pretend you didn't hear anything, ok?"


"Wait! What am I supposed to do? What is going on?! Tell me something!" he yelled back.


"Oh dear, see? This is why! Look, just keep doing what you're doing, get that compass, and make your way to the truck okay? Things will become more clear then. That's all I can say!"


He opened his mouth to respond, but he felt a tangible pop as if a microphone had been switched off or unplugged. It was like the voice had hung up on him? This was getting too weird.


Cas slipped the makeshift knife into his equally makeshift belt, and slid the tool roll into one of the pockets of his cargo pants. He kept the gun out pointed in front of him. He kept walking and soon came across more of the large eyeless bugs.


They had their heads down and were chomping away on… a human body. He recoiled, and then froze. There were three of them, all with their mandibles covered in blood,  but none had seemed to notice him yet. He was about to try and sneak his way around them, but something that voice had said came back to him. "Get the compass." He needed to level up.


He pointed his pistol at the closest bug and pulled the trigger. The red light burned right through it, and the bug immediately went slack. The last ring on the barrel went dim and as he pulled the trigger at the next one, nothing happened, other than the metal cylinder at the back clicking and a crack appearing. There was no time to inspect it though, he had to get the bugs while they were distracted.


Pulling his knife, he ran up to the second one, and stabbed the tip down at the bug but it left little more than a chip in the top shell. As the bug began to turn towards him, he jammed the knife back at it, this time aiming for the gap between its head and its large back plate. It worked. As the chitinous shiv slid in deep, the bug went slack.


The final bug was moving, but to Cash's shock, it had started to eat the first bug he'd killed! He began to run up to stab it, but something caught his eye and he had an idea. Instead of swinging down, he kept moving forward and kicked his foot up to catch the soft underside of the bug. The kick sent it spinning to land a couple feet away on its back. He dove for it before it could right itself, stabbing the dagger into its gut and wrenching up towards the head. The gooey guts squirted up at him and splashed across his face as he felt the knife skip and skate across the underside of the hard top shell of its carapace. He could feel the warm goop drip down his face as the bug stopped twitching.


Cas straightened himself and did his best to wipe his face clean. Like using ember on a Squirtle, it wasn't very effective. He looked back at the body, and took a few deep breaths. He wouldn't last long if he didn't adapt. Sorry dude, whoever you were. He found an unopened pack of tissues in the pocket of the guy's leather jacket and used it to finish up what else he could from his face and hands. While the man was fully dressed, to Cash's dismay, he was at least a size too small and clearly none of the clothes or shoes would fit him. However, he did find something helpful.


Clutched tight with rigor mortis in the man’s left hand was a long army green sheath made of that stuff that cops’ gun holsters were made out of, *kyogre or something like that?* he asked himself. Drawing out the blade, he discovered it was a long black machete roughly 45cm long, with coarse, saw-tooth serrations down most of it’s spine.


The wide, flat sheath fit terribly in his hand so carrying it was out. He tried to shove it in to his makeshift belt, but the width of the sheath strained the plant fibers too much and they snapped. He thought to take the guy’s belt, but it had been eaten through by one of the scavenger beetle things.


“Shit, sorry man. Look, you’re not going to be using it anyways right? Come on, don't look at me like that!” Cas said to the corpse, as if it was somehow listening. He gingerly pulled off the man’s leather jacket, doing his best to avoid touching the bloody, pulpy areas. Laying it out on the ground, he cut it into long strips with the machete to make a mostly functioning belt that he tied with a knot around his waist. Lastly he took the man's shoe laces and used them to tie the sheath to his new, horribly misshapen belt and belt loops.


"I desperately need to get proper clothes. If this is really like a game, maybe there'll be a shop or something." He said to himself.


…


Over the next thirty minutes, he kept walking and killed any bugs he came across, eventually hitting level three. Soon he came to a fork in the path, the first deviation he'd found since entering the woods from the same, arrow-straight path. A sudden noise made him whip his head up. He had been in the middle of fiddling with his ray gun, trying to see how to get it to fire again, or charge or whatever, and when he heard the scream, he spun to face it, with the ray gun pointed at whatever had made the noise, it's uselessness forgotten. He pulled the trigger only to have the gun click uselessly in his hands. He tossed it aside in frustration at the gun, and in exasperation at himself.


Pressed up against a tree was a young kid who barely looked a day over sixteen, if that. He was holding one of the bugs away from himself at arms length as it tried lazily to snap and poke at him. The kid had no idea that he was holding the thing completely under his own power, and it was completely helpless. Instead, they looked terrified, as if the bug could get to him at any second.


The tableau was almost comical. "Hey kid, you're fine. The thing can't touch you." Cas said to the screaming teenager, just loud enough to be heard over the witless panicking.


The boy let out a loud yelp, and threw the bug away from himself, effortlessly tossing it across the clearing as if it were no more than a basketball. Cas walked over to the bug as it struggled on its back, and drove his machete through its chest to kill it in one lazy stroke.


"W-who are y-you?" The boy asked, his voice shaking so much Cas thought he was going to piss himself.


"Just breathe bud, the thing couldn't touch you. Don't you have a weapon yet?" Cas replied as he begrudgingly walked over to pick up his useless ray gun.


"My friends call me Cash, what about yours?" he asked, extending a hand down to help the boy up.


"I… don't have any. My name is Sawyer." the kid said dejectedly. "I got this staff in that beginner box, but I don't really know how to…" Suddenly Sawyer darted around behind the tree and Cas could hear the wet retching as Sawyer threw up.


"Sorry about that… I got the staff, but I have no idea how to use it. The description said it was a 'conduit for magical energy'? But like, I don't have magic right?"


"I don't have a clue how to answer that now if I'm being honest," Cas said, scratching his head. "Are you ok there kid? That really didn't sound good."


"I tried eating some food shortly after I woke up, and it immediately made me sick. I threw up so hard I nearly passed out. It was one of those pink pear looking fruits?" It sounded just like the ones he'd seen, but he knew too many horror stories about hikers eating bad fruits to try any wild plant he didn't know.


"I've been throwing up every few minutes since. I think the beetles are attracted to it or something? Before I manage to catch my breath, a handful of them always show up." He could believe it. The kid looked exhausted, and not just from the fear.


"I'm eighteen by the way, I know I look young, but I'm not a kid. Or mostly. Sort of…" Cas quirked a questioning eyebrow at him in amusement and he quickly deflated. "…Tomorrow is my eighteenth birthday," Sawyer muttered under his breath, clearly embarrassed. "I was waiting outside my dorm for my dad to pick me up, and suddenly I was in that white room. We were going to go to a theme park."


Cas suddenly felt guilty, and he didn't even know why, but the sadness radiating off him was heartbreaking. He'd woken up in the room, he couldn't imagine what it would have been like to have been awake when it happened.


" I'm sorry that happened bud, if it's any consolation, I was sleeping when it got me. went to bed feeling good, woke up in a creepy-ass white room, and it full-on dropped me on my ass when it disappeared." Cas said.


Sawyer let out a softhearted laugh. "I guess that does make me feel a little better."


The kid looked idly down at the staff in his hand and then over to the laser pistol Cas had discarded earlier. He'd honestly forgotten about the useless thing until now. He moved to pick it up. "As much good as it will do for me, this thing is dead, it was spent after 3 shots and it doesn't seem to recharge." Cas said, shaking his head grumpily.


"It doesn't have some sort of power cell, or something? What's happened when you've poked around with it?" Sawyer asked. Cas wanted to smack himself in the forehead so hard right then. He loved fiddling around with random tech. It was half of what he did in his spare time now that he worked mostly from home. *Or at least I used to.* Looking closer at it, Cas turned it over and over in his hands, poking at this and that. The only moving part that he noticed was the trigger.


Then he brushed his fingers over the silver cylinder at the back. He felt the slightest wiggle on the flat end of the cylinder as he moved his thumb back and forth over it, Just like where the positive terminal of… a D battery would be. *Am I really that dumb?* He pushed down on the small nub and the chrome cylinder popped open immediately. The metal "*cracked"* perfectly down the middle, and the right half sprung out, splitting again. In the blink of an eye, the smooth cylinder had become a hand crank, just like on a pepper grinder or an old-fashioned hand drill.


"You have *got* to be kidding me." Cas took the end of the crank and hesitantly began to turn in, holding it as far away from his face as he could.


\---


It turned out to be a completely "simple" crank charger of all things. it didn't explode in their faces either, which was good because it took a good minute or so to charge up one shot. As he cranked, the rear most unlit disc flickered and came to life until it was fully charged, then the next flickered on.


To add extra salt to the wound, Cas received a **[ That was embarrassing! ]** achievement and equally named item box that contained none other than an identical "crank cell" as he'd started to call it in his head. **[ Manual Charge Adaptive Backup Cell, Grade 1 ]** was way too long of a name anyways. He nearly threw it into the bushes out of spite but he thought better of it and shoved it in his pocket. *Maybe I can get some money for it, if this thing has a shop or whatever.*


“Actually, can I try charging that?” Sawyer asked.


“Yeah sure, why not.” Cas replied, tossing him the irritating metal cylinder.


\---


As the two of them walked down the road, Sawyer kept looking at his wooden staff. It was a simple enough wooden stick about 6 feet long, and it curled into a sort of shepherd's crook at the top. Its deep brown bark had an assortment of white markings, woven thread, and small green baubles adorning it. He could probably use it as a respectable quarterstaff if he wanted, but  Sawyer didn't seem the type to get into the thick of a melee.


Cas realized he didn't even ask the kid if he wanted to join him, nor did the kid ask. He just started following behind like a stray and Cas hadn't thought much about it. *Yesterday, I'd probably try and shoo the kid off, but who knows what's going to happen next? And frankly, I could start learning to be less of an ass…*


Cas aimed his pistol at a Beetle on a nearby tree and pulled the trigger. The shiny bug thumped to the ground with a smoking hole going through its back right out its underbelly. He'd been practicing with the gun on any beetle they'd passed so he could hopefully improve his aim. It was surprising how easy folks made it look to shoot things accurately in the movies. Or maybe he just sucked because the closest thing he'd fired to a real gun was a nerf pistol or when he'd gone to laser tag as a kid, back when he could still walk. "So what's the description it gives you? The system or whatever must have said something to give you a hint of how to use it, right?" The charging crank popped back out and he set to work rewinding the gun all over again.


"It said something about spirit or energy or whatever but I didn't really get it? Do you know how to use it?" Sawyer held out the staff to him, which he took. The "tooltip" popped up immediately.


"It looks like you have to cast spells with it or some shit? what have you tried?" Cas asked as he handed it back to Sawyer. "Also, did you grow up in a bayou or whatever? What was that bit about?"


"I have no clue," he replied. "I grew up in Southern California, half of my life's been spent under a freaking drought! but I did try some stuff? You know, pointing it at a tree and shouting stuff; **'attack!'**, **'magic!'**, **'cast!'**, like what am I supposed to do?"


Cas thought for a moment." Have you tried… *willing*… it to happen? like pushing your will through it? actually *feeling* the energy and willpower go through you into it?"


Sawyer looked at him like Cas had just tried to sell him on dianetics or something. "Seriously! It's something they had us do in rehab. think about what you want, and *will* it to happen. *Feel* your will flow through what you're focusing on. Try it with the staff." The look continued.


"Not that kind of rehab! Physical therapy. I…" Cas said, gesturing down at his legs. "I got run over by a trust-fund douchebag drunk driving in his daddy's BMW. That was the technique I was using when I finally got my legs to move again for the first time. Definitely more of a curse than a blessing though."


He told Sawyer about the injury as they walked, and Sawyer spent the time he wasn't asking questions staring down at his staff. While getting his legs crushed wasn't fun, it was always pretty fun to see people's reactions when they saw all the scars up and down his legs, and this time was no different.


They continued on for a while longer, chatting idly as they went. Sawyer kept fiddling with his staff and Cas kept picking off the increasingly common beetles. As he fired the last shot in the gun, and missing, again, Sawyer made a comment about feeling defenseless. He had no idea how to fight and the staff barely helped with it in the last fight, and Cas had three shots before he had to run in and try and finish the bugs off with the machete.


“Hold that thought!” Cas said as an idea came to him. He shoved the empty gun into his pocket and pulled out the machete. Running up to the bug now outlined in a trio of smoking holes, he knocked it off the tree with a branch laying nearby and kicked it over with his foot.


Sawyer asked what he was doing, his curiosity quickly turning into disgust as Cas knelt down and split the bug’s abdomen open from tail to tip. Next, he jammed the claw-knife into the gap between the soft “flesh” and the hard shell and wrenched it back and forth causing green goo to ooze out. Jamming his hand down into the sticky goo of the widened gap, he pulled and twisted, slowly but surely separating the guts of the (finally dead) beetle from its hard chitinous shell.


“Seriously, I’ve done dissections in class, and that is absolutely disgusting! What are you actually doing?” Sawyer asked as he covered his mouth, clearly trying not to throw up.


“You said you felt defenseless right? Well here’s the solution!” Cas replied, quite satisfied with himself.


A few minutes later, the separated shell from the beetle had been scrubbed “clean” with nearby mosses and handfuls of dirt, and strapped to Cash’s forearm with a couple lengths of his makeshift cord. He’d originally offered it to Sawyer, but the kid refused and said that Cas was likely the one to need it more. Instead of being disgusted with all the bug guts, to his surprise, Sawyer had actually been keenly interested on inspecting the corpse and shell, poking around the different parts of the beetle, prodding its mouth open, looking for eyes, and more. He even asked Cas to make him one of the claw knives after tearing off one of the sharp digits himself.


Once they were all sorted, they carried on down the path, Cas taking more pot shots at the beetles, and Sawyer continuing to stare down at his seemingly mundane wooden staff  before getting frustrated and simply stabbing one of the bugs that skittered towards him to death with his new knife. Soon enough they came to another clearing, even larger than the one they had met each-other in, and standing directly in the middle of it, clustered up like an impenetrable barrier, were three beetles. But not beetles like they’d seen and killed dozens of so far. Standing at least to his waist, maybe even more, like something out of the BBC’s “Walking with Monsters”, they were the largest beetles he had ever seen. And they were turning right towards them. .


“Well, fuck.”
