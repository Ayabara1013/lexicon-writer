# Chapter 2 - Achievements?

“Huh? Level up? I didn’t…” Cas trailed off as the text in front of him changed. As he watched, it seemed to scratch itself out and write an update, almost as if someone lazily used an old asset as a placeholder and forgot to change it for release.

Cas swatted away the notifications as he read them. "Might as well I guess," he said to himself. "Receive rewards?" Suddenly a brown cardboard box popped into existence in front of him with a shimmer of blue lights. As he crouched down to open it, he immediately recognized it even before reading the printed green text on the side saying "Uhaul Heavy Duty Medium Box".

"Damn, they really are everywhere."

Unable to see anything else he could cut the tape with, he reached over and ripped off one of the bug's legs and used it to slice the tape down the middle. Inside, he found a piece of paper folded in thirds and sealed with a small piece of scotch tape. It sat atop the most beautiful thing he'd ever seen. Beautiful. Beige. Boring. Mundane. Simple. Human. Pants. Oh and also a shirt. But PANTS. Have you ever tried pants? Pretty awesome invention right?

Ye.

Pants.

They were loose, "athletic fit" cargo pants that looked like they were picked up from an army surplus store. After looking at the tag on the inside, he realized that's exactly what they were. It made some sort of sense he guessed.

The shirt was a wrinkled and dilapidated tshirt from a band he'd never heard of called "Darcy Penciltop and the Eraser heads". It looked like it had fallen into disrepair more from being left in a middle aged dad's basement for a couple decades than from actually being worn at all. Taking a closer look at it, there were clear signs of cheap and uneven screen printing as if someone in the band had done it themselves, and likely without any prior experience.

On the back there was a list of tour venues that was only four locations long, all in the middle of Idaho based on the little state map that'd been screen printed underneath the enlarged words in an attempt to fill in the empty space. The last one had even been crossed out with one of those shiny silver signature markers, as if the band had canceled the last show because no one was showing up, and the roadie, (aka. the drummer's little brother) had to go and cross out the venue on every single shirt by hand.

He slipped the clothing on. The pants felt rugged and durable, and the shirt was actually surprisingly comfortable and soft. Cas had got some tshirts made up for his super smash brothers club in college and he recognized the shirt blank when he looked at the label. There'd been a good year or so where he'd worn nothing but bulk ordered blanks of that very shirt, thinking he was so clever and saving so much money. In reality he'd spent close to $300, and people kept asking him if he only owned one shirt because they were all the same colour.

He checked the box just to double check things, and that's when he saw the strangest item in the bunch. It looked like a vintage Flash Gordon-inspired toy ray gun, sitting alone at the bottom of the box. It had a papaya orange grip and trigger guard topped by a tarnished brass ovoid housing. Three silver discs or rings wrapped around the front end, and a long silver barrel extended after that, finishing in a grape-shaped muzzle break. A shark-fin shaped protrusion on top divided the gun in half and behind it was a strange cylinder protrusion that looked something akin to a perfectly smooth chrome D-cell battery. On the right face of the gun's housing was some old engraving that had been so worn away, all he could make out was "R… R…".

As soon as he picked the gun up, the three rings began to glow, causing him to immediately drop it back in the box and jump away. They instantly went dim again, back to their original chrome finish.

He cautiously leaned forwards and picked it back up. They came back to light.

Cas did this again a handful of times, until he was sure it wasn't about to explode in his hands. The light was steady, like a charge indica… He pointed it at the nearest tree and pulled the trigger. A thin beam of red light zipped out and hit the black bark, causing a small explosion of sparks and light.

"A charge light, eh? Ok," Cas said to himself with an excited chuckle. Blasters were *cool*! "That seemed a little weak though?" Looking at the blast mark on the tree, the damage from the bolt was barely any larger than a golf ball. He couldn't really pull out his phone and look up Star Wars clips on YouTube though could he? *Would it? Where even are we?*

He looked around and saw the remnants of the giant bug. There was a large hand sized segment of its shell left untouched by the stomping. Pointing the blank spot, he pulled the trigger again and the Star Wars looking bolt of red energy flashed out and burned right through the carapace, leaving a smoking hole. This time, the blast hole still had a roughly four centimeter diameter, but unlike before there was a clear hole at least half that across and with cracks and burns spreading out even further.

“How the heck does that work?” As he looked closer at the tree, it looked like the bark acted as a sort of ablative armour, flaking away bit by bit and dissipating the heat and energy. *Remembering that for later!*

Unfortunately, he also noticed the rings on his blaster. Two of the three rings had gone dim, with only the largest one, closest to his hand remaining lit. He had one shot left. And at least as far as he could tell, no way to recharge it. Balls.

“Okay, what do I do now?”

Cas shook his head, and rubbed wearily at his face. He had no idea what was going on, and every time something happened, things just seemed to get more and more confusing. Honestly, one thing that had started to nag at him was that he had yet to see a single other person, aside from those first couple of moments.

There was nothing to do but keep moving. He looked at his makeshift club and found it wanting. It was a good club, but at least against these resilient-ass bugs, it didn't do much. Kneeling down, he picked up the bug's fore leg claw thing. While the angle of the "blade" on its inside edge was too sharp to slash anything well, its serrations might cut somewhat, and it could definitely pierce.

Also, still no damn shoes. Maybe there'd be some in a loot box maybe? Did this thing have loot drops?

He headed further into the forest, only stopping briefly to strip some long fronds from a couple of trees. One time when he was little, his shoes got soaked when he fell into a stream. He'd been trying to cross it on a dead tree that had fallen across. He'd had his arms stuck out to the sides like a tight rope walker, and his tongue sticking out of his teeth. The first part he'd seen when his dad had taken him to the circus, but he didn't even realize he was doing the second part. When he fell, he'd scraped his knee on the river rocks.

He cried like the eight year old boy he was. ie, a lot.

His grandmother patched him up, showing him some herbs and flowers he could smush up in his palm and press to the scrapes. She washed the scrapes out with some clean water from her bottle, and pressed the paste to the scrapes and held them down with a certain leaf, which she tied on with a length of some fiber she stripped off another plant.

"Gammy Jay" had been like that. She grew up out in the woods, and taught him everything she knew. It's what made him want to be a forest ranger in the first place. He remembered all the lessons she'd taught him, especially when (usually often) he'd needed to do it a few times to actually get it right.

He braided some of the strips together and made himself a belt, and then wrapped the rest of the strips around the bug claw as a basic handle. Hopefully it'd stop the thing from cutting into his palm, at least for a little bit.

Cas wiped his eyes after reading that item description, and quickly realized that he never even had an uncle Ted. And then suddenly, the bizzare item description narrator suddenly jumped back to usual too-casual blood thirsty announcer voice. Almost as quickly as the emotions had hit him, they had fled as if they'd never been there. It felt like he'd been enchanted somehow. In fact, he'd bet money on it. "What the hell? Are you guys trying to give me whiplash here? What was the point of that?" he asked the empty air.

"What the fuck??!"
